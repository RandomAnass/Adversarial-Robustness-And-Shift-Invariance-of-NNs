\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!3,colframe=blue!40!black,boxrule=0.5pt,arc=2pt,left=4pt,right=4pt,top=3pt,bottom=3pt]
\small
\textbf{Summary.} Whether shift-invariance helps or hurts adversarial robustness is contested: Ge et al.\ (2021) prove a fully shift-invariant classifier can be far less robust than a fully connected one, while anti-aliasing work reports the opposite correlation. We organise both regimes around one quantity. A shift-invariant linear classifier separates classes only through their projection onto the invariant subspace, with an exact margin identity (the distance between the projected convex hulls); a convolution-plus-pooling layer with a nonlinearity instead computes power-spectrum features, and a degree-three bispectrum invariant separates even the localized, phase-coded case that the power spectrum cannot. The surviving robustness is summarised by a margin-to-Lipschitz ratio $\eta/L$, a Lipschitz--margin certificate (no general converse) that also predicts trained-model robustness. A capacity-matched dissection on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10 confirms that $\eta/L$, not shift-consistency, predicts the adversarial robust radius (Pearson $0.998$ on CIFAR), and decomposes each invariance operator's effect into a margin term and a Lipschitz term: anti-aliasing acts on the Lipschitz term, exact invariance costs margin. Under PGD adversarial training a threat-matched $\eta/L$ predicts AutoAttack robust accuracy (Pearson $0.90$) while shift-consistency anti-predicts it ($-0.88$). On the optimization side we prove shift-invariance is margin-free on orbit-closed data, so gradient descent self-symmetrizes; it does help on near-orthogonal cluster-geometry data, where the trajectory-level selection theorem remains open.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section*{1\quad Problem and motivation}
Image classifiers are often called shift-invariant if circular translations of the input do not change the prediction. The published evidence on what this does to adversarial robustness is mixed. Ge et al.\ (2021) show a fully shift-invariant network needs an $\ell_2$ perturbation of order $1/\sqrt{d}$ to be fooled, while a fully connected one needs order $1$. Anti-aliased pooling work (Zhang 2019; Saha and Gokhale 2024) reports that operators which increase shift-invariance also increase robustness. A separate line proves a trade-off between geometric and adversarial robustness (Kamath et al.\ 2021; Tram\`er et al.\ 2020). A practitioner is left without a rule for when invariance is safe.

\textbf{What is already known.} Invariant and equivariant architectures and their margins (Cohen and Welling 2016; Lawrence et al.\ 2021; Chen and Zhu 2023), invariant scattering and bispectrum features (Bruna and Mallat 2012; Kakarala 2012), Lipschitz--margin certification (Hein and Andriushchenko 2017; Tsuzuku et al.\ 2018), and implicit-bias non-robustness (Frei et al.\ 2023; Li et al.\ 2024) are all relevant pieces, but none ties shift-consistency to the adversarial radius through a single measurable quantity.

\textbf{The gap.} There was no account that (i) says exactly what discriminative signal survives a given invariance, (ii) turns that into a robustness certificate, and (iii) is validated against a standardized strong attack at matched capacity. This project supplies all three.

\section*{2\quad Method}
\textbf{Structural core.} For a finite symmetry group acting orthogonally, an invariant linear classifier sees the data only through the group-average projector $P_G$; its maximum $\ell_2$ margin is $\tfrac12\,\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{conv}\,P_GX_+,\mathrm{conv}\,P_GX_-)$ (the cyclic-shift case is the DC projection). A single convolution, square, and global-average-pooling layer computes power-spectrum coordinates; a degree-three bispectrum invariant additionally separates phase-coded classes such as the Ge black/white dot $\pm e_j$ that the power spectrum cannot.

\textbf{The criterion.} The surviving robustness is summarised by $\eta/L$: the margin $\eta$ of the data in the invariant feature space divided by the Lipschitz constant $L$ of that feature. The bound $r_2\ge\eta/L$ is a one-sided Lipschitz--margin certificate; we show there is no general converse, so $\eta/L$ is a certificate and a predictor, not a characterization. A companion orbit-flip radius $\rho_G$ bounds the excessive-invariance (semantic) side, recovering Tram\`er et al.

\textbf{Experiments.} A training-free test computes the achievable separation by classifier family. A capacity-matched dissection trains four architectures (standard, anti-aliased, exact cyclic, shift-augmented) with identical parameter counts, measuring the per-sample $\ell_2$ robust radius with a decoupled direction-and-norm minimum-norm attack (validated against the closed-form linear radius to under one percent) and the margin/Lipschitz decomposition. A PGD-adversarial-training arm makes AutoAttack informative. A synthetic optimization study compares unconstrained and weight-tied ReLU networks on orbit data.

\section*{3\quad Main empirical evidence}
The training-free test confirms the structural predictions exactly: the single dot is separable by an unconstrained classifier, weakly by linear invariance, not at all by the power spectrum; orthogonal-frequency data is separable by neither linear classifier yet separable by the power-spectrum invariant.

\textbf{$\eta/L$ predicts robustness; shift-consistency does not.} Across capacity-matched cells, $\eta/L$ tracks the robust radius (Spearman $0.96$ on MNIST, $0.71$ on Fashion-MNIST; Pearson $0.998$ on CIFAR-10), while shift-consistency is uncorrelated or mildly anti-correlated. The exactly invariant model has shift-consistency $1.0$ yet the smallest robust radius.

\textbf{Under adversarial training.} PGD-AT at $\ell_\infty{=}8/255$ makes AutoAttack informative ($27$--$41\%$ robust accuracy, no gradient masking). Shift-consistency still anti-predicts robustness (Pearson $-0.88$), and the threat-matched ratio $\eta/\|\nabla M\|_1$ (the $\ell_\infty$ dual) predicts AutoAttack robust accuracy (Pearson $0.90$), while the $\ell_2$ ratio does not, because adversarial training robustifies in $\ell_\infty$.

\section*{4\quad Mechanism diagnostics}
The margin/Lipschitz decomposition separates two mechanisms. Relative to the standard baseline, anti-aliasing leaves the margin essentially unchanged and lowers the Lipschitz constant ($\Delta\log L\approx-0.2$), buying robustness through reduced input sensitivity. Exact cyclic invariance instead shrinks the margin ($\Delta\log\eta\approx-0.3$ to $-0.4$) and loses robustness, the real-data form of the Ge collapse. This is the structural criterion made operational: invariance helps robustness only when it does not cost margin.

\textbf{Optimization.} On orbit-closed data we prove shift-invariance is \emph{margin-free}: the invariant and unconstrained two-layer-ReLU max-margin values coincide (orbit-averaging an unconstrained solution gives an invariant one of equal minimum norm). A synthetic test confirms unconstrained and tied networks reach the same robustness on generic orbit data, while on near-orthogonal cluster-geometry data the tied network reaches a $20$--$60\%$ larger $\eta/L$, most in the lazy regime. So whether invariance re-points the implicit bias is data-geometry-dependent.

\item \textbf{Structural theory}, corrected after an external audit and re-verified: invariant margin as projected-hull distance (general finite group), the $\eta/L$ certificate with an explicit no-converse counterexample, the power-spectrum and bispectrum span lemmas, the exact orbit-rank lemma, and the orbit-flip bound.

\item \textbf{Optimization sprint}: a margin-free theorem on orbit-closed data, a synthetic unconstrained-versus-tied study showing the data-geometry dichotomy, and explicit ReLU KKT systems for the open selection problem.

\end{itemize}

\section*{6\quad Future work}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.05em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Cluster-geometry selection theorem.} Prove that on near-orthogonal cluster-geometry orbit data gradient flow selects a higher-$\eta/L$ invariant solution, using the explicit KKT systems and the frequency-domain reduction; the lazy regime shows the largest empirical gap.
\item \textbf{Bispectrum certified robustness.} Turn the bispectrum separation of phase-coded classes into a certified-radius construction, trading the larger Lipschitz constant against the recovered margin.
\item \textbf{Scale.} Larger backbones and stronger adversarial-training recipes, to check whether the threat-matched $\eta/L$ law and the consistency anti-prediction persist at RobustBench scale.
\end{itemize}

\section*{7\quad Project context}
This grew out of a TU Munich practical course that reproduced and extended Ge et al.\ (2021). The supervisor's framing asked for a comparison against four papers: Ge et al.\ (2021), the two Kamath et al.\ trade-off papers, and Galloway et al.\ on batch normalization; the dissection reproduces the Galloway effect under fixed-$\epsilon$ accuracy and shows it is threshold-dependent under the radius metric. Repository: \texttt{adversarial-robustness-shift-invariance/}; paper and proofs report under \texttt{paper/report/}. Compute to date is a few GPU-hours on two RTX A6000.
