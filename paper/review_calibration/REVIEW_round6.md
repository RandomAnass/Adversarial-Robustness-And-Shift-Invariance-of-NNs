## Summary

The paper reconciles two opposing claims — that shift-invariance *reduces* adversarial robustness (Ge et al. 2021) and that anti-aliasing (more shift-invariance) *improves* it — by arguing both are regimes of one scalar, the margin-to-Lipschitz ratio `η/L` of the invariant feature. Contributions: (i) an exact identity for the invariant linear margin as a projection/hull separation (Thm 1) plus a finite-group version (Thm 2); (ii) a Lipschitz–margin certificate `r₂ ≥ η/L` completed to a conditional two-sided bracket `η/L ≤ r₂ ≤ η/α` with condition number `κ` (Prop 1); (iii) a conv+quadratic+pool = power-spectrum lemma (Lemma 1) with reconciliations of Ge (`γ_inv = 1/√d`) and Kamath (labeled "an interpretation"); (iv) a lazy/NTK cluster theorem (Thm 5) that invariance lowers the certified Lipschitz constant; (v) a large, capacity-matched empirical dissection over three datasets, two threat models, scaling to PreActResNet-18 (with an adaptive-polyphase exact-invariance mechanism), with per-sample certificate, bootstrap CIs, and gradient-masking checks, showing threat-matched `η/L` predicts the robust radius / AutoAttack accuracy while shift-consistency does not (and anti-predicts under AT).

**New since round 5 (the two things I am asked to judge):**
- **(a) The "impossibility" overclaim is walked back.** Theorem 3 (the homogeneity/Euler envelope `‖∇M‖ ≥ M/‖x‖`) is now stated as a *loose structural floor*, explicitly "loose at the operating point," not an impossibility proof. The training no-go is attributed to the scale-degeneracy lemma (Lemma 2) plus four controlled interventions (§5), with Thm 3 as an idealized mechanism that does not literally cover the BN/bias ResNet models.
- **(b) An actionable selection rule is added (p. 14).** Across five conditions (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 ℓ∞, CIFAR-10 ℓ₂, and a six-operator graded anti-aliasing sweep), choosing the invariance operator by attack-free `η/L` picks the most robust operator every time (zero AutoAttack regret against the oracle), while choosing by shift-consistency picks the *least* robust (mean regret 8.9 pp, up to 13). The paper honestly adds that clean accuracy is *also* a near-zero-regret selector, so the demonstrated point is the *failure of consistency*, not a margin of `η/L` over clean accuracy.

**My verdict on the central question.** Both changes are genuine, responsive improvements and they move the paper *up within* the borderline band — the walk-back removes the single soundness objection I raised in round 5, and the selection rule converts the abstract "consistency fails" claim into a concrete, usable rule. But they do **not** move the *contribution category* off borderline into a confident accept. The selection rule's honest tie with clean accuracy means the paper still offers no positive lever that beats a free, already-standard baseline; what is delivered is a sharpened *negative* result (don't select invariance operators by shift-consistency) on top of a literature reconciliation and a diagnosis. That is precisely the profile the corpus gates against without a champion. Net: a half-step up from round 5, to the accept side of the borderline, but still accept-if-championed.

## Strengths

- **S1 — Reconciles a real, citable contradiction.** Placing Ge (2021) and the anti-aliasing line on one axis (`η/L`) is a genuine conceptual contribution, not a restatement. The Ge recovery, the power-spectrum sign-blindness argument, the bispectrum escape, and the Kamath "small-`η/L` corner" reading are clean and correct. This kind of unifying account is the part a sympathetic reviewer (the M9SKazbVkJ / C36v8541Ns spirit, where "an empirical/analysis paper need not have a method") will champion.
- **S2 — The round-5 down-scoping is a real credibility gain.** In round 5 the headline "we prove this coupling is structural… an impossibility result for training `η/L`" was an overclaim (loose cap ~`sup‖x‖`, ~tens, vs operating `η/L` ~0.08–0.15; lockstep binds only at `∇M ∝ x`, which trained models violate). The paper now states this plainly — Thm 3 is a "loose floor," exact only for bias-free homogeneous nets, "does not literally cover" the BN/bias ResNet-18 (Remark 1) — and re-routes the no-go to Lemma 2 + the four interventions. This is exactly the scope-honesty the paper already applied to Kamath ("interpretation") and the bracket ("conditional"). Honest scoping is rewarded in this corpus and was the single thing I docked under W2 last round; it is fixed.
- **S3 — Evaluation soundness is a differentiator.** AutoAttack ≤ matched PGD for every arm and every condition (no gradient masking; Table 3 text reports a mean abs radius-vs-AA gap of 0.014, max 0.025); per-sample certificate Spearman 0.91–0.95, positive in all 36 models; bootstrap CIs and permutation tests. This matters concretely: the nearest method-bearing reject in the corpus, **DUCAT (sBpYRQOrMn)**, had a method with significant gains and post-rebuttal scores of 6,6,6,5 yet was **rejected** because its robustness evaluation did not include an adaptive attack (possible gradient masking). This submission's controls are the kind of rigor whose absence sank that paper.
- **S4 — The negative finding about shift-consistency is useful and now operational.** "Shift-consistency does not predict robustness — and anti-predicts under AT — while threat-matched `η/L` does" is more actionable than typical critique results because shift-consistency is a *widely used* invariance diagnostic. The new selection demonstration (p. 14) turns this into something a practitioner can act on, even if the positive selector ties clean accuracy. The threat-matching insight (`ℓ₁` gradient for `ℓ∞` training) is a real refinement.
- **S5 — The four-intervention loop is clean and falsifiable.** Theory predicts collapse → all four first-order penalties collapse the margin, AutoAttack flat-to-down, no gradient masking (Fig 8). This is rarer than it sounds and directly supports the (now correctly attributed) training no-go.

## Weaknesses (ranked, located)

**W1 — The actionable rule ties the free baseline, so it sharpens a negative result rather than supplying a positive lever. [p. 14 "A practical reading"; §5]**
This is the decisive issue and it is the crux of the round-5 fix #2. The ask was a demonstration that selecting by `η/L` beats selecting by shift-consistency *and yields a real robustness gain a practitioner could not get for free*. The paper delivers the first half (8.9 pp over consistency, zero regret) but the paper itself states clean accuracy is also a near-zero-regret selector. So the operational consequence reduces to "do not use shift-consistency to choose invariance operators" — a genuine, useful negative, but the *positive* selector `η/L` is matched by clean accuracy, which is free and universally computed. Every accept in the corpus offers a lever that beats the relevant cheap baseline: M9SKazbVkJ's AR-AT beats TRADES/LBGAT; C36's LVM-RS beats RS and Lipschitz-nets; xDxskDUvte's G-CNN beats the standard CNN; dT0eNsO2YLu's LPS beats APS (and survives a capacity-matched control). Here the relevant cheap baseline (clean accuracy) *ties*. By the corpus logic this lands the paper with the honest-analysis rejects: **aS2Yl8s5OG** (exemplary presentation, 2×7s, surprising findings, no method that beats the cheap baseline → rejected, "no champion / marginal contribution") and **KX5hd1RhYP** (correct first-of-kind negative result → rejected, "technically limited / too narrow / workshop"). The paper is richer than both, which is why it should not fall *below* the borderline — but the tie keeps it from clearing it.

**W2 — It is a selection among the paper's own arms / five conditions, not a held-out architecture or task. [p. 14; Tables 3–6]**
The "five conditions" are the same architecture-by-width arms and the graded sweep already used for the correlations, re-read as a selection problem. There is no demonstration on an unseen architecture or task where acting on `η/L` produces a robustness improvement. So the rule is an internal re-summary of the existing dissection, not external validation that the diagnosis transfers. This is the difference between "the diagnostic is consistent with my own data" and "a practitioner deploying by `η/L` on a new model wins."

**W3 — Contribution category is unchanged: reconciliation + diagnosis + negative result, no method that improves a metric. [§1 contributions; §4 "open selection problem"; §7]**
§4 is explicit that whether gradient descent reaches the robust invariant solution is left open (a "trajectory-level implicit-bias theorem … left to future work"), and §5 confirms `η/L` "cannot be trained." So the paper is, by its own framing, a diagnosis plus an open problem. The corpus is consistent that this category clears a top main track only when paired with a method/positive mechanism (all four accepts) or, failing that, needs a designated champion (which aS2Yl8s5OG did not get even with 2×7s).

**W4 — Several headline correlations still rest on small grids / range-widening. [§5 "Statistical power"; Fig 2/4]**
The four capacity-matched arms *alone* give an anti-prediction of only `-0.43` (n.s.); it "sharpens to `-0.81` only once the two low-invariance arms widen the consistency range." The 24-cell standard grid gives consistency CI `[-0.68, +0.09]`, p=0.11 (n.s.), while `η/L` is `+0.998`; the 12-cell AT grid gives consistency `-0.75` CI `[-0.92, -0.02]` p=0.006 and matched `η/L` `+0.83` CI `[+0.49, +0.93]`. The effects are real and the AT anti-prediction is now significant, but the strongest consistency claims still appear once the design is widened, which a skeptical reviewer will probe. Single author; no ImageNet (acknowledged, §7).

**W5 — Much of the theoretical scaffolding is assembled from known ingredients. [§3.2–3.4, App. A.1]**
The Lipschitz–margin certificate is classical (Hein 2017, Tsuzuku 2018, by the authors' own statement); the bracket's upper arm is conditional on a co-Lipschitz hypothesis close to assuming a reaching path with a lower-Lipschitz bound; the lazy-cluster theorem rests on Elesedy projection + smooth activation and is explicitly open for ReLU; Thm 1's hull form, Lemma 1's span, and the orbit-rank propositions are modest, and the authors say so. The genuinely new theory is incremental; the load is carried by the empirics and the reconciliation.

**W6 — The abstract is still a single ~400-word run-on. [Abstract; §1]**
The presentation bottleneck from round 5 is largely unaddressed at the top: the abstract stacks "co-Lipschitz," "orbit-closed," "threat-matched," "self-symmetrizes," "homogeneity envelope," "scale-invariant" before any are defined, in one paragraph. C36v8541Ns shows reviewers penalize exactly this ("verbose… I struggled a lot") — that paper still got in, but only because it carried a method through the fog. Tables/figures are clear; the prose front door is not.

## Questions

1. **Does `η/L` ever beat clean accuracy as a selector?** The honest tie is the load-bearing limitation (W1). Is there any regime — e.g., where clean accuracy and robustness *decouple*, such as across very different capacities, or under distribution shift / corruption — where selecting by `η/L` picks a more robust operator than selecting by clean accuracy? A single condition where the two cheap baselines disagree and `η/L` wins would convert the diagnosis from "explanatory correlate of clean accuracy" into "tool that adds value."
2. **Held-out validation.** Can you exhibit one unseen architecture or task where a practitioner who deploys the highest-`η/L` operator obtains a measured robustness gain over the consistency-chosen (and clean-acc-chosen) operator? This is the external check W2 asks for.
3. **Robustness of the consistency anti-prediction to grid size.** Given the four-arm `-0.43` (n.s.) vs the widened `-0.81`, what is the smallest balanced design (equal arms across the consistency range, not range-widened) for which the AT anti-prediction is significant? Please report it directly rather than via the widened grid.
4. **Thm 3 reach on the real models.** You now say Euler is broken by BN/biases. Can you report the Euler residual `⟨∇M,x⟩ − M` on the trained PreActResNet-18 models, to quantify how far the "loose floor" is from binding? This would make the down-scoping concrete rather than asserted.
5. **The bracket's `α`/`κ` for the trained ResNets.** Is the co-Lipschitz `α` (hence `κ`) available empirically for the ResNets, or only for the linear / power-spectrum surrogates where the bracket is exact?

## Overall rating

**6 / 10 — marginally above the acceptance threshold (ICLR 1–10 scale; 6 = marginal accept, 5 = marginal reject, 8 = accept).**

This is a genuine half-step up from the stable round-1–5 borderline-5, and I want to be precise about why it moves and why it does not move further. **It moves** because the two round-6 changes are real and responsive: the walk-back removes the soundness objection that was the explicit basis of my round-5 deduction (a credibility gain, not cosmetic), and the selection rule is a concrete operationalization that directly answers the *first half* of round-5's fix #2. **It does not move further** because the selection rule's honest tie with clean accuracy means the paper still has no positive lever that beats a free baseline — what is delivered is a sharpened negative result plus a reconciliation, which is the category the corpus rejects without a champion (aS2Yl8s5OG rejected at 2×7s; KX5hd1RhYP rejected as "too narrow"). A reviewer who values clean diagnoses and accepts "an analysis paper need not have a method" (the M9SK/C36 spirit) can defend 6; a results-focused reviewer applying the corpus gate strictly will hold at 5 and, absent a champion, the no-champion path lands on reject. I land at a soft 6: accept side of the borderline, accept-if-championed.

**Confidence: 4 / 5.** I read the submission end-to-end including the appendix, checked the Euler/Cauchy–Schwarz derivation, the bracket proof, the lazy-cluster proof, and the table numbers, and I am familiar with the Ge / anti-aliasing / Lipschitz-margin literature. I am not certain how a panel would weigh the reconciliation-plus-diagnosis contribution against the still-absent lever.

## Decision and top-3 fixes

**Decision: borderline accept (accept-if-championed).** The honest down-scoping of Thm 3 is a clear credibility gain and the selection demonstration is a real, concrete actionable, so the paper has moved to the accept side of the borderline. But the actionable consequence ties clean accuracy and so is a sharpened negative result, not the practice-changing lever that distinguishes every corpus accept; without a designated champion this remains at risk of the aS2Yl8s5OG outcome.

1. **Break the tie with clean accuracy — this is the single thing that would convert it to a clear accept.** Find or construct one regime where clean accuracy and robustness disagree and `η/L` selects the more robust operator (e.g., across capacities, under corruption/distribution shift, or on a held-out architecture). One such condition turns `η/L` from "explanatory correlate" into "tool that adds value beyond what is free," which is exactly the lever that separates the corpus accepts (M9SK, C36, xDxskDUvte, dT0eNsO2YLu) from the rejects (aS2Yl8s5OG, KX5hd1RhYP).
2. **Validate the diagnosis out of sample.** Move at least one selection result off the paper's own arms onto an unseen architecture/task, so the rule is external validation rather than an internal re-summary (W2).
3. **Rewrite the abstract and intro as plain prose, and report the two honest measurements (Euler residual on the ResNets; smallest balanced grid for which the AT anti-prediction is significant).** Two-sentence legible thesis; define `η/L`, "threat-matched," "exact-cyclic" before use; cut the 400-word run-on (W6). The two measurements convert the now-honest caveats from assertions into evidence (W3, W4).

**Best home if it stays diagnosis-only:** a strong TMLR or workshop paper (the explicit recommendation that landed KX5hd1RhYP and, in spirit, aS2Yl8s5OG). To clear a top-tier main track with confidence, it needs fix #1.
