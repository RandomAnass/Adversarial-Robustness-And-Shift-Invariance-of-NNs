# Review — "When Does Shift-Invariance Permit Adversarial Robustness? A Margin-Preservation Theory"

**Round 4. Rating scale: ICLR 1–10** (6 = marginally above acceptance threshold, 5 = marginally below, 8 = accept). Calibrated against the supplied corpus, with the closest anchors being NeurIPS-2025 `xDxskDUvte` (the competing "Bridging Symmetry and Robustness" spotlight), ICLR-2024 `C36v8541Ns` (Lipschitz–Variance–Margin, accept), ICLR-2025 `M9SKazbVkJ` (invariance regularization in AT, accept), and the two honest-but-rejected analysis papers ICLR-2025 `KX5hd1RhYP` ("ACR is a Poor Metric") and NeurIPS-2023 `aS2Yl8s5OG` ("AT without Perturbing all Examples").

---

## Summary

The paper argues that the contradictory literature on shift-invariance and adversarial robustness — Ge et al. (2021), invariance *reduces* robustness, versus the anti-aliasing line, invariance *improves* it — describes two regimes of a single quantity: the margin of the discriminative signal preserved after projection onto the invariant feature space, divided by that feature's Lipschitz constant (η/L). Contributions: (i) a structural theory (Thm 1: the invariant linear margin equals the DC-projection separation; Thm 2: the finite-group generalization; Lemma 1: a conv+square+pool layer computes power-spectrum features; recoveries of Ge 2021 and a reinterpretation of Kamath et al. 2021 as a small-η/L corner); (ii) the Lipschitz–margin certificate r₂ ≥ η/L completed to a conditional two-sided bracket η/L ≤ r₂ ≤ η/α (Prop 1, App. A.2); (iii) an optimization section showing invariance is "margin-free" on orbit-closed data, with a lazy/NTK cluster theorem (Thm 4) proving invariance lowers the certified Lipschitz constant at equal margin; (iv) an extensive empirical program: a training-free structural test, a capacity-matched architectural dissection on MNIST/Fashion/CIFAR-10, PGD-AT across three datasets and two threat models scaling to PreActResNet-18, a per-sample certificate, a graded anti-aliasing control at matched clean accuracy, η/L as an attack-free selection signal, and a controlled causal intervention showing that regularizing the threat-matched margin gradient *raises η/L exactly as predicted yet does not improve robustness* because the margin collapses in lockstep — concluding η/L is a diagnostic, not a training target. The headline empirical claim is a dissociation: shift-consistency does not predict adversarial robustness (and anti-predicts it under AT), whereas a threat-matched η/L does.

This is a fourth review round; prior rounds reached a stable borderline (≈5, "accept if championed"), with the chief criticism being "rigorous diagnosis without a method." The new material is the graded control with paired significance, the selection signal, and the honest negative intervention.

---

## Strengths

1. **A real, cited confusion is genuinely organized.** The Ge-2021-vs-anti-aliasing tension is a recognized and unresolved point in the literature (both directions are actively published, including the `xDxskDUvte` spotlight). Putting them on one axis — *is the discriminative signal preserved by the invariant projection with positive margin* — is a useful reframing, and the recoveries (Thm 1 → Ge's Corollary 1; the power-spectrum sign-blindness explaining why both the Ge dot and the Kamath construction sit at η/L≈0) are clean and correct.

2. **The empirical dissociation is the load-bearing and most valuable finding.** That shift-consistency (a diagnostic practitioners actually use) carries essentially no information about the robust radius (Spearman −0.31/0.00 in Table 2; bootstrap CI [−0.68,+0.09], p=0.11 on the 24-cell grid) while a margin/sensitivity ratio carries it, and that consistency *anti*-predicts under AT, is a non-obvious, practically relevant message delivered across datasets, threat models, and a ResNet backbone.

3. **Evaluation rigor is above the bar set by several accepted papers in the corpus.** Per-sample robust radius via DDN validated against the closed-form linear radius to <1%; explicit gradient-masking checks (AutoAttack ≤ matched PGD reported throughout, including the non-differentiable adaptive-polyphase arm); bootstrap CIs, permutation tests, paired t-tests, and partial correlations controlling for clean accuracy. By contrast, `xDxskDUvte` and `M9SKazbVkJ` were accepted while *promising* error bars in revision. The per-sample certificate (Spearman ≈0.91 AT / 0.95 standard, positive in all 36 models) is a strong robustness check on the central proxy.

4. **Intellectual honesty is consistently demonstrated.** The paper flags the near-tautology of the η/L–r₂ correlation itself ("high by construction... the across-cell face of the per-sample certificate"), the clean-accuracy confound on the exact-cyclic arm ("cannot be separated from a clean-accuracy deficit"), the absence of a universal converse, and the open rich-regime trajectory. The negative intervention (Fig 7) is reported plainly rather than buried. This is the standard one wants and is rarer than it should be.

5. **The proofs I checked are correct.** Thm 1 and Thm 2 (App. A) are correct (group-average projector, convex-hull margin). Thm 4 (lazy-cluster, App. A.2) is a correct application of the Elesedy-2021 invariant-RKHS projection to the certified Lipschitz constant, and the smooth-activation caveat (Remark, citing Bietti–Mairal that bare-ReLU NTK is non-Lipschitz) is exactly the honest qualification a careful reader would demand. The Kamath reinterpretation is correctly downgraded to "an interpretation... a self-contained restatement would be needed to make it a theorem."

## Weaknesses (ranked, located)

1. **No method, and the one method-shaped experiment is a negative result — this is the decisive contribution gap (whole paper; §Experiments, Fig 7).** The corpus is unambiguous about the bar in this subfield. Every accepted analog ships a working improvement: `xDxskDUvte` (spotlight) has an architecture that raises robustness without AT; `C36v8541Ns` improves certified radii (LVM-RS beats both RS and Lipschitz nets); `M9SKazbVkJ` has AR-AT beating baselines on the robustness–accuracy trade-off — and its reviewers explicitly tolerated thin theory *because the method worked*. The two closest pure-analysis papers were rejected: `KX5hd1RhYP` ("ACR is a poor metric") was correct, technically sound, and agreed-upon, yet rejected as "limited technical contribution / too narrow / better as a workshop"; `aS2Yl8s5OG` had two 7s and surprising findings yet was rejected because "no reviewer stepped forward to champion the paper." This submission is in that family. The honest intervention sharpens the science but does not change the contribution arithmetic: the paper's own conclusion is that its central quantity *cannot* be used to train, and the sole actionable deliverable (η/L as an attack-free selection signal, §"A practical reading") is thin — it ranks operators a practitioner could equally rank by running AutoAttack on a handful of checkpoints, and the operator it crowns (anti-aliasing) is already known to help. A results-focused reviewer may read the negative intervention as *confirming* the weakness rather than mitigating it.

2. **The headline η/L→robustness correlation is partly true by construction, and the paper says so (Tables 2–3, Figs 4–5, §Statistical power).** η/L is a first-order *lower bound* on r₂; a Pearson of 0.998 against r₂ under standard training largely measures local linearity, not a discovery — the paper concedes this. The non-tautological content reduces to (a) the dissociation against consistency (genuinely valuable, see Strength 2) and (b) the AT correlations against AutoAttack (0.88 ℓ∞-CIFAR). But even (b) is close to a restatement that "robust models have large logit margin and small input-gradient norm," which is near the definition of local robustness and is the decades-old Lipschitz–margin story the paper itself attributes to Hein–Andriushchenko/Tsuzuku. The reviewer is left asking what is predicted that the certificate did not already give. This is the same critique `KX5hd1RhYP` drew ("the result is a direct consequence of Φ⁻¹'s bounded domain") and it was fatal there.

3. **The consistency *anti*-prediction is materially confounded by the invariance↔fitting trade-off, which the paper concedes for its strongest single data point (§"graded control"; Tables 5, A6).** The exact-cyclic arm — the outlier driving the negative correlation in the 8-cell tables — also has the lowest clean accuracy (0.57–0.63 vs 0.69–0.78), and the paper states directly that "the exact-cyclic arm's robustness deficit cannot be separated from a clean-accuracy deficit." The mitigations (partial correlation −0.82, low-invariance arms widening the consistency span at ResNet scale, the matched-clean-accuracy graded sweep) are the right moves and partly work, but the cleanest "low consistency + high robustness" evidence rests on two added arms, and across the graded family "clean accuracy predicts robustness as strongly as η/L does." So the deeper claim "invariance per se anti-predicts robustness" is not cleanly separated from "exact invariance underfits."

4. **The "one quantity" unification is weaker than advertised because the mechanism is not invariant across settings (Figs 5–6, §"graded control").** Anti-aliasing helps by *lowering L* (standard training, Fig 5) but by *raising η* (AT, matched-clean-accuracy sweep). Because anything affecting robustness must, by the certificate, move η or L, attributing every effect to "the η/L criterion" risks being a re-description rather than a predictive law. The framework explains *post hoc* which term moved; it is not shown to predict *which* term an unseen operator will move.

5. **Individually, the theory is modest; the value is synthesis (App. A).** Thm 1 is essentially Ge's DC-projection observation in convex-hull form. Prop 1's upper arm (the "conditional converse") is near-definitional: defining α as a co-Lipschitz constant *along a path that reaches the boundary* makes η ≥ α‖γ(T)−x‖ almost immediate, so the bracket is more a careful bookkeeping of the Tsuzuku certificate than a new result; its non-trivial content (κ=1 for linear, exactness via Hein) is borrowed. Prop 2 (orbit-flip) is a one-line restatement of excessive-invariance attacks. Thm 4 is an application of Elesedy-2021. None is individually a strong theorem; reviewers who weight theoretical novelty (as `C36v8541Ns`'s reviewers did, scrutinizing every proof step) will find the synthesis valuable but the parts familiar.

6. **Threat-matching is only decisively load-bearing in one of four conditions (Table 4).** Matched vs mismatched η/L are near-equal on MNIST (0.92/0.90) and on ℓ₂-CIFAR the *mismatched* ratio is marginally higher (0.88/0.91). Matching clearly matters only for ℓ∞-CIFAR. The causal intervention (Fig 7) nicely shows the *matched* norm is the one that moves the ratio, but the empirical case that matching is *necessary for prediction* is thin and slightly oversold in the abstract.

7. **Presentation is dense to the point of obstructing the contribution (abstract; throughout).** The abstract is a single ~400-word paragraph; sentences routinely run 50+ words with stacked clauses; the contribution list interleaves "proved," "open," "conjectured," and "companion technical report." `C36v8541Ns` was hammered repeatedly for wordiness and still got in, but it had a method to anchor the reader. Here, where the contribution is conceptual, the writing makes the (real) message harder to extract than it should be on first read.

## Questions

1. Beyond the attack-free selection signal, is there any setting in which η/L yields an *actionable* gain — e.g., does selecting an operator by η/L on a held-out family beat selecting by clean accuracy alone? Across the graded family you state clean accuracy predicts robustness as strongly as η/L, so what does η/L add operationally?
2. Can you isolate the consistency anti-prediction from underfitting *without* the exact-cyclic arm at all — i.e., restrict to the low-invariance + anti-aliased + shift-aug arms (all at comparable clean accuracy) and report the consistency-vs-robustness correlation? If it survives there, that is the cleanest possible evidence and would substantially strengthen the paper.
3. Under AT, is η/L→AutoAttack (0.88) predicting anything the input-gradient ℓ₁ norm alone does not? A partial correlation of AutoAttack with η/L controlling for L (and with L controlling for η) would show whether the *ratio* matters or only its denominator.
4. Prop 1: at γ(T) you have g=0, whose sign is neither +y nor −y; the inf-radius bound is fine as a limit, but please confirm the co-Lipschitz hypothesis is verifiable (not merely assumable) for any non-linear feature beyond the power-spectrum surrogate, since otherwise the bracket is vacuous off the linear case.
5. For ℓ₂-CIFAR, given that matched and mismatched ratios are near-proportional, what is the operational claim of "threat-matching" — is it falsifiable in a regime where the gradient norms are not proportional and you predict a divergence?
6. The negative intervention used a single ℓ₁ margin-gradient penalty. Did any reparameterization (e.g., penalizing L while *constraining* η ≥ η₀, i.e., enforcing the absolute margin the self-limiting coupling erodes) break the lockstep? A null there would make the "diagnostic-not-target" claim much more robust; a positive there would be the missing method.

## Scores

- **Soundness: 3 (good).** Proofs checked are correct and honestly scoped; the empirical methodology is careful (gradient-masking checks, CIs, permutation/paired tests, DDN validation). Held below 4 by the partly-tautological central correlation and the conceded clean-accuracy confound on the key anti-prediction.
- **Presentation: 2 (fair).** Strong figures/tables, but a single-paragraph mega-abstract and very long sentences obscure an otherwise extractable message.
- **Contribution: 2 (fair).** The reconciliation and the consistency⊥robustness dissociation are genuine and useful (a high "2," arguably a low "3"), and the reframing of the `wang2025bridging` spotlight as "necessary but not sufficient" is well-aimed. Held at 2 by the absence of any method/improvement, the by-construction component of the headline result, and the individually-modest theory — the exact profile the corpus rejects when no one champions it.

## Overall rating: **5 / 10 (marginally below the acceptance threshold)**  — ICLR 1–10 scale.

**Confidence: 4 / 5.** I am familiar with the Lipschitz–margin/certified-robustness and invariance literatures and checked the appendix proofs; residual uncertainty is on how an AC weighs a high-rigor, high-honesty analysis paper with no method.

This is a strong borderline, and my number matches the prior rounds' equilibrium for a defensible reason rather than inertia. The round-4 additions — the matched-clean-accuracy graded control (a real strengthening of the "invariance can help, through the margin" claim, with proper paired significance), the selection signal, and the honest causal intervention — improve the paper's scientific quality and credibility, and the causal validation that *only* the threat-matched norm moves the matched ratio is a genuine, satisfying confirmation. But they do not move the contribution verdict, because (a) they add no winning method, (b) the negative intervention's own conclusion is that the paper's central quantity is not a usable training target, which to a results-oriented reviewer reinforces rather than resolves the standing "diagnosis-without-a-method" objection, and (c) the most novel empirical claim remains partly entangled with the invariance↔fitting trade-off the paper itself flags. Against this corpus, the paper sits above `KX5hd1RhYP`/`aS2Yl8s5OG` on breadth, rigor, and reconciliation value, but below the accepted `xDxskDUvte`/`C36v8541Ns`/`M9SKazbVkJ`, all of which carried a working improvement. It is precisely the "accept-if-championed" profile: an AC who values a careful negative-result-plus-reframing will (and should) be able to push it to a 6; an AC who applies the field's default "interesting analysis, limited actionable contribution" will land it at reject, exactly as happened to the two closest analogs. The honest negative result helps the paper's integrity and slightly helps me; it does not, by itself, clear the bar.

## Decision and top-3 fixes

**Decision: borderline reject (5), championable to accept.** To convert the borderline into a clear 6+:

1. **Add a method, or make the selection signal demonstrably actionable.** Either (i) find a training reparameterization that breaks the self-limiting coupling (Question 6 — constrain absolute margin while penalizing sensitivity) and yields even a small AutoAttack gain over vanilla AT, which would turn the paper from diagnosis into method and almost certainly clear the bar; or (ii) prove the selection signal earns its keep with a held-out-family experiment where picking by η/L beats picking by clean accuracy. Without one of these, the paper remains in the rejected-analysis family regardless of polish.

2. **Decisively disentangle the consistency anti-prediction from underfitting.** Report consistency-vs-robustness on a subset of arms held at matched clean accuracy *excluding* the exact-cyclic outlier (Question 2), and report the η/L→AutoAttack partial correlation controlling for L alone (Question 3). If the dissociation survives both, foreground it as the paper's primary result and demote the by-construction η/L–r₂ numbers, which currently invite the "tautology" critique.

3. **Rewrite the abstract and introduction for a first-read contribution.** Break the abstract into ≤4 sentences leading with the one finding a reader should keep (consistency does not predict robustness; a threat-matched margin/sensitivity ratio does), and separate "proved / measured / open" cleanly. Given the conceptual nature of the contribution, presentation is doing real damage here, and this is cheap to fix.
