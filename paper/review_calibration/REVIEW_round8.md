# Review — When Does Shift-Invariance Permit Adversarial Robustness? A Margin-Preservation Theory

Reviewer emphasis (as assigned): (i) is the theory correctly positioned against prior work, with nothing standard over-claimed as novel; (ii) is each theoretical result tied to an empirical verification; (iii) do the experiments support the abstract's claims. Calibrated against the 34-thread corpus (notably the NeurIPS-2025 equivariance spotlight `xDxskDUvte`, the ICLR-2024 Lipschitz–Variance–Margin poster `C36v8541Ns`, and the rejected understanding-only paper `aS2Yl8s5OG`).

---

## Summary

The paper proposes that the two opposing literatures on shift-invariance and adversarial robustness — Ge et al. (2021), where forced invariance collapses robustness to `O(1/√d)`, and the anti-aliasing line where more invariance helps — are two regimes of one quantity: the margin of the discriminative signal that survives projection onto the invariant subspace, divided by that feature's Lipschitz constant (`η/L`). It develops: an exact identity for the invariant *linear* margin as the separation of the DC-projected data (Thm 1) and its finite-group generalization; a conditional two-sided bracket `η/L ≤ r₂ ≤ η/α` (Prop 3); a lemma that conv+quadratic+pooling computes power-spectrum features (Lemma 2); recoveries of Ge (2021) and a reinterpretation of Kamath et al. (2021); a radial–tangential split of the margin gradient for homogeneous networks (Prop 6) with an "achieved-margin" envelope (App. Prop 9); an orbit-rank characterization; and a lazy/NTK cluster theorem (Thm 2). Empirically it runs a capacity-matched architectural dissection (FC / single-conv-invariant / multi-conv; anti-aliased / exact-cyclic / shift-augmented) on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, under standard and PGD adversarial training, two threat models, and a PreActResNet-18 backbone, plus four interventions that try (and fail) to train `η/L`.

The central empirical message: shift-consistency does **not** predict adversarial robustness (and *anti*-predicts it under adversarial training), whereas a threat-matched `η/L` does; exact invariance hurts by collapsing the margin while graded anti-aliasing helps; and `η/L` is a diagnostic, not a trainable target.

## Strengths

- **A genuinely useful corrective, correctly scoped.** The finding that shift-consistency anti-predicts adversarial robustness under AT (`-0.88` against AutoAttack, Table 5; Fig. 6) while a *threat-matched* `η/L` predicts it is a real, somewhat counterintuitive result, and it is not a strawman: the paper is careful that anti-aliasing helps through the **Lipschitz term**, not consistency ("Anti-aliasing leaves the margin essentially unchanged and lowers the Lipschitz constant"). Unifying the Ge-2021 "harms" result and the anti-aliasing "helps" result under one criterion is a clean conceptual contribution.
- **Unusually honest about what is classical.** The Lipschitz–margin certificate is explicitly attributed: "The Lipschitz--margin certificate `r₂≥η/L` is classical \citep{hein2017formal,tsuzuku2018lipschitz}... Our contribution is not the bound but the shift-invariance dissection it supports," and Corollary 1 says "This is the standard Lipschitz-margin bound." This is the right posture and avoids the over-claiming the corpus penalizes (cf. the "Breaking Inherent Trade-Off" overclaim flagged in `sBpYRQOrMn`).
- **Theory attributions are accurate** (verified). Soudry (2018) for GD → hard-margin direction giving `κ_i = ‖x_i‖/γ` in the linear case; Lyu-Li (2020) / Ji-Telgarsky (2020) for directional convergence to a KKT point of the margin program; Vardi-Shamir-Srebro (2022) for ReLU KKT points not being globally max-margin — the paper correctly uses the **achieved** margin `γ_IB` rather than the global one and says so explicitly: "the bound cannot use the global margin but does hold with `γ_IB`." Elesedy (2021) for orbit-averaging as an orthogonal projection, and the Bietti-Mairal caveat that the ReLU-NTK feature map is not Lipschitz (Remark 1) are both correct and honest.
- **Statistical hygiene above the corpus median.** Bootstrap CIs over resampled cells, two-sided permutation tests, partial correlation controlling for clean accuracy (`-0.82`), explicit gradient-masking checks (AutoAttack ≤ matched PGD throughout), and denser 24-/12-cell grids to address the small-`n` worry. This directly preempts the "no error bars / too few seeds" complaint that recurs across the corpus (`xDxskDUvte`, `M9SKazbVkJ`).
- **Breadth.** Three (four with CIFAR-100) datasets, two threat models, two backbones, standard + AT. That is broader than several accepted corpus papers, and the ResNet exact-invariance result is reproduced through a *second* mechanism (adaptive polyphase sampling), which is a nice robustness check.

## Weaknesses (quoted and verified)

**W1 — The paper's own disclosures show `η/L` has no decision-value over clean accuracy, which hollows out the "selection rule" contribution.** Quoting the selection-rule paragraph: "Clean accuracy is an equally good selector (`0.08` points of regret), consistent with the margin being the common driver... the operative point is the failure of consistency, not a margin of `η/L` over clean accuracy." Verified: the abstract itself hedges "`η/L` (like clean accuracy) picks the most or near-most robust one." So the practical artifact on offer (an attack-free selector) is matched by the trivially cheaper baseline of clean accuracy. The paper's defense — that the margin is the upstream quantity both depend on — is scientifically reasonable, but it converts a claimed *practical* contribution into a *mechanistic* one. A reviewer will ask: what does `η/L` let me do that clean accuracy does not? On the current evidence, nothing. This is the single biggest threat to perceived contribution and maps directly onto the corpus pattern where understanding-only papers with unclear actionable value land at borderline-to-reject (`aS2Yl8s5OG`).

**W2 — The headline `η/L`↔`r₂` correlation (Pearson 0.998) is near-tautological, and the paper says so.** Quoting the statistical-power paragraph: "The `η/L`--radius value is high by construction: `η/L` is a first-order lower bound on `r₂` and these models are close to locally linear, so this correlation is the across-cell face of the per-sample certificate... rather than a separate finding." Verified. This is admirably candid, but it means the most eye-catching number in Figure 4 (0.998) is essentially `r₂ ≈ η/L` measured on near-linear models, not independent corroboration. The abstract's "the threat-matched ratio predicts the adversarial robust radius" leans on this near-identity for the standard-training case. The load-bearing claim is the *dissociation* (consistency carries no information, `η/L` carries it), and the writing should foreground that rather than the 0.998.

**W3 — The recently added two-sided bracket (Prop 3) is near-tautological in proof and loose-to-vacuous on the actual models.** The upper arm's proof (App. B) assumes "a continuous path... with a co-Lipschitz (lower-Lipschitz) bound `|g(γ(t))-g(x)| ≥ α‖γ(t)-x‖` holding up to `t=T`" and then reads off `‖γ(T)-x‖ ≤ η/α`. That is essentially assuming the conclusion (a path along which the score descends at rate ≥ α reaches the boundary at distance ≤ η/α). The content therefore rests entirely on whether `α` is a meaningful, computable constant — and that is verified tight only on a toy power-spectrum surrogate ("realized `κ≈1.6`--`2.4`"). On the trained networks the same envelope constant is `κ≈26` (Fig. 3 caption: "at the trained networks the tangential factor is large (`κ≈26`)"). With `κ≈26`, the upper arm `r₂ ≤ η/α = κ·(η/L)` permits `r₂` up to ~26× the lower bound, i.e. the "characterization up to `κ`" is effectively vacuous in exactly the regime the paper cares about. I did **not** find an error in the proof; the critique is about strength/novelty of the contribution, not correctness. As stated, the bracket is a modest add-on dressed as a "conditional converse" that turns "the one-sided certificate into a two-sided bracket"; the honest reading is that it is a two-sided bracket only where `κ` is small, which is not the trained-network regime.

**W4 — Prop 6 (the "exact polar identity") is elementary; its weight is carried by the interpretation, not the identity.** The split `∇M(x) = m(u)u + ∇_S m(u)` with `‖∇M‖ = (M/‖x‖)√(1+‖∇_S log m‖²)` is the standard radial/tangential decomposition of the gradient of a degree-1 homogeneous function (the radial part is Euler's theorem). The proof is correct. Calling it "an exact polar identity" and a Proposition is acceptable, but a skeptical reviewer will note it is freshman calculus restated, and that the genuine content is (a) identifying the tangential factor with the data condition number and (b) the achieved-margin envelope (App. Prop 9), which *is* a careful and correctly-attributed result. I would recommend leaning on (a)/(b) and presenting Prop 6 as a remark, to avoid the impression of inflating elementary material into a theorem (the corpus rewards calibrated framing).

**W5 — The consistency "anti-prediction" is fragile within the controlled comparison and depends on adding non-capacity-matched arms.** Quoting §5: "Across the four capacity-matched arms alone the anti-prediction is weak and not significant (`-0.43`); it sharpens to `-0.81` only once the two low-invariance arms widen the consistency range. This is the point rather than a caveat." Verified, and the mechanism (consistency saturates to `[0.93,1.00]` on a strong backbone) is plausible and well-argued. Still, the strongest negative numbers (`-0.81` to `-0.88`) come once arms beyond the matched four are included, and the exact-cyclic arm is a consistency-1.0 / low-robustness outlier. The paper handles this with partial correlation and the MNIST saturation discussion, but a reviewer should weigh that the dramatic anti-correlation is partly a function of arm selection. Relatedly, the graded-sweep paragraph concedes "the exact-cyclic arm's robustness deficit cannot be separated from a clean-accuracy deficit" — so on six arms "clean accuracy predicts robustness as strongly as `η/L`," reinforcing W1.

**W6 — Net contribution is a diagnostic / negative result, and the paper says so.** "So `η/L` diagnoses robustness rather than serving as a training target" (abstract), backed by four interventions that all collapse the margin (App. F). This is good science and honestly reported, but combined with W1 (no edge over clean accuracy) and W2 (the predictive correlation is near-identity), the deliverable reduces to: a conceptual reframing + a corrective to the anti-aliasing reading of shift-consistency. That can be enough at a top venue (the equivariance paper `xDxskDUvte` got a spotlight on a *positive* architectural story with weaker statistics), but understanding-only papers without a sharp, surprising, *standalone* take-away are the ones that stall in this corpus (`aS2Yl8s5OG`, reject). The paper needs the reframing to be unmistakably the headline.

**Minor / could-not-fully-verify**
- The bispectrum claim ("separates `±e_j` with margin `d^{-3/2}`") is stated without proof or experiment. It is plausible (the bispectrum is odd-degree in the spectrum, hence sign-sensitive; coefficients scale as `d^{-3/2}` under the `1/√d` normalization), but it is not load-bearing — fine to leave, but mark it clearly as a claim.
- Prop 2 (general finite-group margin) and Prop 4 (orbit-flip `ρ_G`, the excessive-invariance bound) have no empirical counterpart. They are modest structural extensions; not a strong weakness, but they are theory-only relative to the abstract's "each result tied to verification" spirit.
- The Cohen (2019) gloss "the largest the class probabilities alone can certify and is exact for linear bases" slightly conflates Cohen's worst-case-over-classifiers tightness with a "linear basis"; the worst case is realized by a linear half-space, so the phrasing is defensible but could be tightened.

## Questions for the authors

1. **Where, if anywhere, does `η/L` beat clean accuracy as a selector?** Is there a regime (e.g. arms that decouple margin from fit, or fixed-clean-accuracy families beyond the graded sweep) where `η/L` and clean accuracy diverge and `η/L` wins? Without such a case, what is the operational claim for `η/L` over clean accuracy?
2. **For the bracket (Prop 3): on the trained CIFAR/ResNet models, what is the measured `α` and hence `κ`?** If `κ≈26` there, in what sense is "`η/L` determines the robust radius up to `κ`" a useful characterization rather than a restatement that the lower bound is loose?
3. **Is the consistency anti-prediction robust to dropping the exact-cyclic arm entirely?** I.e. across only the anti-aliased family + low-invariance arms (excluding the consistency-1.0 outlier), does the negative correlation survive?
4. Under adversarial training the `ℓ₂` radius "decoupled," so the standard-training `η/L`↔`r₂` near-identity (W2) no longer holds and you switch to threat-matched `η/L`↔AutoAttack. Can you state precisely which predictive claim is independent corroboration versus a near-definitional consequence, per condition?

## Overall score: 5 / 10 (marginally below the acceptance threshold; a sympathetic reviewer could reach 6)

Justification, calibrated to the corpus. This is careful, honest, broad, and statistically disciplined work — clearly above the typical reject in rigor and well above it in calibration of claims (it never commits the over-claiming that sank `sBpYRQOrMn`, and its error-bar/seed hygiene is better than the spotlight `xDxskDUvte` at submission). The unifying `η/L` framing and the consistency-anti-predicts-robustness corrective are real contributions. What holds it at the borderline rather than above it is that the paper's own (commendable) disclosures dissolve much of the headline: `η/L` is no better than clean accuracy as a selector (W1), its strongest correlation is near-tautological (W2), the recently added converse is loose precisely on the models studied (W3), and the central deliverable is ultimately a diagnostic/negative result whose unique value over simpler quantities is not established (W6). In the corpus, understanding-only papers with a sharp positive story can win (`xDxskDUvte` spotlight) while those whose practical value reviewers cannot pin down stall (`aS2Yl8s5OG` reject, 7/5/5/7/5). This paper sits between: stronger theory and rigor than the latter, but a thinner standalone payoff than the former. I land at 5, leaning to 6 if W1 is answered with a concrete `η/L`-beats-clean-accuracy result.

## Single highest-value change

Demonstrate a regime where `η/L` provides decision value that clean accuracy does **not** — e.g. a family of operators (or a fixed-clean-accuracy sweep) in which clean accuracy is flat or misleading while threat-matched `η/L` still orders robustness correctly. Absent that, reframe the paper so the *dissociation* (shift-consistency is uninformative / anti-predictive for adversarial robustness, with exact invariance hurting through margin collapse) is unambiguously the headline contribution, and explicitly demote (i) the 0.998 `η/L`↔`r₂` correlation to "near-tautological cross-check" and (ii) the two-sided bracket to a small-`κ`-only result. This directly addresses W1, W2, and W6 — the three weaknesses most likely to cost the paper its champion.
