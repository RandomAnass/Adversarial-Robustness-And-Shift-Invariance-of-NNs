# Review — "When Does Shift-Invariance Permit Adversarial Robustness? A Margin-Preservation Theory" (Round 7)

*Reviewer role: NeurIPS/ICLR area reviewer. Scale: ICLR-style 1–10 (8 = accept, 6 = marginal accept, 5 = marginal reject, 3 = reject), the scale used in rounds 1–6. Calibrated against the corpus in `review_calibration/reviews/` — read in full this round: NeurIPS_2025_xDxskDUvte (nearest-topic accept: "Bridging Symmetry and Robustness"), NeurIPS_2023_aS2Yl8s5OG (honest-analysis-no-method reject, 7/5/5/7/5), ICLR_2025_KX5hd1RhYP (first-of-kind negative-result reject, 6/5/3); INDEX + rounds 5–6 for context; I checked the proofs in §3, App. A.1–A.3 and the table numbers myself. ## Summary

The paper reconciles two opposing strands of the literature — that shift-invariance *reduces* adversarial robustness (Ge et al. 2021) and that more shift-invariance via anti-aliasing *improves* it — by arguing both are regimes of one scalar, the margin-to-Lipschitz ratio `η/L` of the invariant feature. The theoretical core is: (i) an exact identity for the invariant linear margin as a DC-projection / convex-hull separation (Thm 1) with a finite-group generalization (Prop 1); (ii) the certificate `r₂ ≥ η/L` completed to a *conditional* two-sided bracket `η/L ≤ r₂ ≤ η/α`, `κ = L/α` (Prop 2); (iii) a conv+quadratic+pool = power-spectrum lemma (Lemma 1) with reconciliations of Ge (`γ_inv = 1/√d`) and Kamath (labeled "an interpretation"); (iv) an exact radial–tangential split of the margin gradient (Prop 4) and a scale-invariance degeneracy (Lemma 2) that together argue `η/L` is a *diagnostic, not a trainable target*; (v) a lazy/NTK cluster theorem that invariance lowers the certified Lipschitz constant (Thm 5). Empirically it is a large, capacity-matched dissection on MNIST / Fashion-MNIST / CIFAR-10(-100), two threat models, scaling to PreActResNet-18 (with a second exact-invariance mechanism, adaptive polyphase), with a per-sample certificate, bootstrap CIs, and gradient-masking checks, showing threat-matched `η/L` predicts the robust radius / AutoAttack accuracy while shift-consistency does not (and anti-predicts under AT), plus four interventions confirming `η/L` cannot be trained.

**My verdict.** The paper is honest, technically sound, well-controlled, and genuinely unifying — clearly richer than the two nearest rejected analogs in the corpus. But its own proposed diagnostic delivers no positive lever that beats a free, already-computed baseline: as a selection rule `η/L` *ties* clean accuracy, and as a training target it provably fails. That is the exact contribution profile the corpus rejects without a champion. It sits right at the borderline; I land marginally below the bar by default, accept-if-championed.

## Strengths

- **S1 — Reconciles a real, citable contradiction.** Putting Ge (2021) and the anti-aliasing line on one axis is a genuine conceptual contribution, not a restatement. The Ge recovery, the power-spectrum sign-blindness argument ("The power spectrum is sign-blind … so even the power-spectrum invariant cannot separate the dot"), the bispectrum escape, and the Kamath "small-`η/L` corner" reading are clean and, where I checked them, correct. A reconciliation is more than the single-phenomenon contribution of aS2Yl8s5OG or the single-metric critique of KX5hd1RhYP.

- **S2 — The proofs I checked are individually correct.** Thm 1 (invariance ⟹ `w ∈ span(1)`, margin = half inter-interval distance), Lemma 1 (Parseval), Prop 4 (degree-1 homogeneity ⟹ `M = r·m(u)`, Euler `⟨∇M,x⟩ = M`, the `√(1+‖∇_S log m‖²)` split), Lemma 2 (scale invariance ⟹ constant-classifier optimum), the orbit-rank lemmas, and the exactly-solvable toy model (App. A.1: `min (a−r)²+r²` at `r = a/2` gives radius `a/√2`) all check out. The lazy-cluster theorem (Thm 5) is a correct application of the Elesedy projection / Pythagorean identity, honestly gated to smooth activations (Remark 1). I could find no flawed step to flag — this is not a "theorem-is-wrong" review.

- **S3 — Evaluation soundness is a real differentiator.** AutoAttack ≤ matched PGD for every arm and condition (no gradient masking, including the non-differentiable adaptive-polyphase arm); the per-sample DDN radius validated against the closed-form linear radius "to under one percent"; the per-sample certificate (Spearman ≈ 0.91–0.95, positive in all 36 CIFAR models); bootstrap CIs + permutation tests; two threat models; a second exact-invariance mechanism at ResNet scale. This is exactly the rigor whose *absence* sank a method-bearing reject in the corpus (DUCAT, sBpYRQOrMn, no adaptive attack).

- **S4 — Honest scoping throughout, and it is now consistent.** The bracket is "under an explicit reachability condition"; Kamath is "an interpretation … a self-contained restatement … would be needed to make it a theorem"; the no-converse fact and normalization-dependence are stated plainly in §7; the round-5 "impossibility" overclaim is gone — Prop 4 is now framed as an *exact* polar identity whose tangential factor is the data condition number, "large at the operating point," which is the honest reading. The corpus rewards this kind of scoping (it is what the aS2Y reviewers praised even while rejecting).

- **S5 — One genuine positive result, correctly attributed to prior art.** The graded anti-aliasing sweep gives "+0.013 (bootstrap CI [+0.011,+0.014])" robust-accuracy gain "at matched clean accuracy … to within one point (range 0.010)," beating the aliased baseline in 39/40 cells. This is a real gain *not* explained away by clean accuracy — but it confirms the Grabinski/Zhang anti-aliasing recipe and the paper's contribution there is the margin-vs-Lipschitz *mechanism*, not a new operator. The four-intervention falsification loop (theory predicts collapse → all four penalties collapse the margin, AA flat-to-down, no masking) is clean and rare.

## Weaknesses (ranked, located)

**W1 — The paper's own diagnostic supplies no positive lever that beats a free baseline; this is the binding constraint, not a soundness defect. [p. 14 selection rule; §5 training; abstract]**
The decisive corpus gate is whether a paper offers something a practitioner could not get for free. Here the proposed tool does not. As a *selection rule*, p. 14 states it plainly: *"Clean accuracy is an equally good selector ($0.08$ points of regret) … the operative point is the failure of consistency, not a margin of $\eta/L$ over clean accuracy."* As a *training target*, §5/App. A.5 show it provably fails: *"$\eta/L$ … cannot be trained: every first-order attempt to raise it collapses the margin, leaving robustness flat or worse."* So what is delivered is a *negative* operational result — do not select invariance operators by shift-consistency — plus a reconciliation and a diagnosis. I verified this is not me reading the paper uncharitably; the paper itself foregrounds it. This is precisely the category the corpus rejects without a champion: **aS2Yl8s5OG** (exemplary presentation, surprising findings, two 7s, no lever beating the cheap baseline → *Reject*, "no reviewer stepped forward to champion … contribution somewhat marginal") and **KX5hd1RhYP** (correct first-of-kind negative result → *Reject*, "technically limited / too narrow / better as a workshop"). The submission is richer than both — which is why it should not fall *below* the borderline — but the tie keeps it from clearing it. This is not a penalty for honesty (the honesty is S4); it is the contribution category the corpus gates on.

**W2 — The abstract sells the selection rule as a positive result and omits the tie the body discloses. [Abstract, line 42]**
The abstract reads *"used as an attack-free rule for selecting an invariance operator, $\eta/L$ picks the most or near-most robust one while shift-consistency picks the least,"* which a reader will take as a positive lever. The decisive caveat — that clean accuracy ties `η/L` (p. 14) — appears only in the body. (To the authors' credit, the §1 contribution bullet does add the parenthetical "*(like clean accuracy)*," so the framing is inconsistent between abstract and intro.) The honest headline is a negative result; the abstract should say so. This is a framing defect, locatable and fixable, not a fabrication.

**W3 — Several headline correlations rest on small grids and on range-widening. [§5 "Statistical power," p. 12; "Scaling," p. 13]**
The four capacity-matched arms alone give a consistency anti-prediction of only `-0.43` (n.s.); it *"sharpens to $-0.81$ only once the two low-invariance arms widen the consistency range. This is the point rather than a caveat."* The reframing is defensible — a near-constant predictor cannot correlate — but a skeptical reviewer will note the strongest consistency claims appear only after the design is widened, and the 24-cell standard grid leaves consistency non-significant (`-0.33`, CI `[-0.68,+0.09]`, `p=0.11`). The AT anti-prediction is now genuinely significant (12-cell `-0.75`, CI `[-0.92,-0.02]`), so this is a "thin in places," not "unsupported." Single author; no ImageNet (acknowledged, §7: *"still larger architectures … are left to future work"*). The nearest accept (xDxskDUvte) was pushed to ImageNet-100 during rebuttal and complied; a reviewer here will ask the same.

**W4 — The flagship `η/L`-vs-radius correlation is, by the paper's own statement, not independent evidence. [§5, p. 12]**
The much-quoted Pearson `0.998` is honestly down-weighted: *"The $\eta/L$–radius value is high by construction: $\eta/L$ is a first-order lower bound on $r_2$ … so this correlation is the across-cell face of the per-sample certificate … rather than a separate finding."* I credit the honesty, but it means the load-bearing empirical claim reduces to the *dissociation* (consistency carries no info, `η/L` carries it), which rests on the smaller-grid significance of W3. The "0.998" should not be read as the paper's evidentiary backbone, and a reader skimming figures might.

**W5 — Much of the theoretical scaffolding is assembled from known ingredients; the genuinely new pieces are modest and the authors say so. [§3.2–3.4, App. A.2]**
By the authors' own statement, *"The Lipschitz–margin certificate $r_2\ge\eta/L$ is classical \citep{hein2017formal,tsuzuku2018lipschitz}."* The bracket's upper arm is conditional on a co-Lipschitz / reaching-path hypothesis that is close to assuming a path to the boundary with a lower-Lipschitz bound; the lazy-cluster theorem rests on Elesedy projection + a smooth-activation assumption and is explicitly open for ReLU; Thm 1's hull form, Lemma 1's span, and the orbit-rank propositions are described as "modest structural contributions." This is the KX5hd1RhYP pattern ("relatively obvious … a direct consequence") — the load is carried by the empirics and the reconciliation, not by new theory. Not a defect, but it caps the "technical contribution" axis.

**W6 — The abstract remains a single ~400-word run-on that front-loads undefined jargon. [Abstract]**
"co-Lipschitz," "orbit-closed," "threat-matched," "self-symmetrizes," "homogeneity," "scale-invariant," "data condition number" all appear before any is defined, in one paragraph. The C36v8541Ns thread shows reviewers penalize exactly this verbosity ("I struggled a lot"); that paper survived because it carried a method through the fog. Tables/figures are clear; the prose front door is the bottleneck. Minor but real, and unchanged across rounds.

## Questions to authors

1. **Can `η/L` ever beat clean accuracy as a selector?** W1 is the whole ballgame. Is there a regime where clean accuracy and robustness *decouple* (across very different capacities, under corruption / distribution shift, or on a held-out architecture) and `η/L` selects the more robust operator while clean accuracy does not? One such condition converts the diagnosis from "correlate of clean accuracy" into "tool that adds value."
2. **Held-out validation.** Can you exhibit one *unseen* architecture or task where deploying the highest-`η/L` operator yields a measured robustness gain over both the consistency-chosen and the clean-accuracy-chosen operator? Right now the "five conditions" on p. 14 are the same arms used for the correlations, re-read as a selection problem (internal re-summary, not external check).
3. **Smallest balanced design.** Given the four-arm `-0.43` (n.s.) vs the widened `-0.81`, what is the smallest *balanced* grid (equal arms spanning the consistency range, not range-widened) for which the AT anti-prediction is significant? Reporting that directly would defuse W3.
4. **The bracket on real models.** Is the co-Lipschitz `α` (hence `κ`) measurable for the trained ResNets, or only for the linear / power-spectrum surrogates where the bracket is exact? If only the latter, say so in Prop 2's statement.
5. **ImageNet feasibility.** Do the two laws (consistency anti-predicts, threat-matched `η/L` predicts) hold on even one mid-scale dataset (e.g., ImageNet-100, as the comparator paper added)? A single positive data point would materially strengthen generality.

## Scores

- **Soundness: 3 (good).** Proofs I checked are correct; the empirics are unusually well-controlled with genuine gradient-masking and per-sample checks; the round-5 overclaim is resolved and Prop 4 is now honestly an exact identity. Residual caveats (small grids W3, the by-construction correlation W4) are honest and minor.
- **Presentation: 2 (fair).** Logically structured, clear figures/tables, honest caveats — but the run-on abstract and the abstract/body framing mismatch (W2, W6) impede a fair first read.
- **Contribution: 2 (fair, bordering good).** A genuine reconciliation plus an operationalized "consistency fails" finding and strong evaluation; capped by the absence of any lever from its own diagnostic that beats a free baseline (W1) and by the assembled-from-known-ingredients theory (W5). Sits between aS2Yl8s5OG (contribution 2s, rejected) and the method-bearing accepts (contribution 3s) — closer to the former in *category*, closer to the latter in *rigor*.

## Overall rating

**5 / 10 — marginally below the acceptance threshold (6 = marginal accept, 5 = marginal reject).**

One-line justification: a rigorous, honest, genuinely unifying analysis with best-in-class evaluation, but no positive lever from its own `η/L` diagnostic that beats a free baseline (it ties clean accuracy as a selector and provably fails as a training target) — the exact profile the corpus rejects without a champion, though richer than the rejected analogs, so not below 5.

I want to be explicit about the 5-vs-6 line and why I sit a half-step below round 6. The two changes round 6 rewarded (the Prop-4 down-scoping and the selection demonstration) are real and I credit them in S4/S5. But reading the *static* paper against the corpus gate rather than against its own trajectory, the binding constraint is unmoved: the selection rule's honest tie with clean accuracy means the deliverable is a sharpened *negative* result, and the two closest analogs — aS2Yl8s5OG with two 7s and KX5hd1RhYP as a correct first-of-kind negative — were both rejected for precisely this, with explicit "no champion / too narrow / workshop" language. A reviewer in the M9SK / C36 "an analysis paper need not have a method" tradition can defend 6; a results-focused reviewer applying the gate strictly holds at 5, and the no-champion path lands on reject. I land at a calibrated 5: accept-side in rigor and breadth, reject-side in contribution category, net marginally below the bar by default.

**Confidence: 4 / 5.** I read the submission end-to-end including the appendix, verified the Euler / Cauchy–Schwarz split, the bracket proof, the toy-model radius, the lazy-cluster proof, and the table numbers, and I am familiar with the Ge / anti-aliasing / Lipschitz-margin literature. I could not independently verify the reported `κ ≈ 26` data-condition-number figure or the exact bootstrap CIs without the data, and I am not certain how a panel would weigh the reconciliation-plus-diagnosis contribution against the absent lever.

## Single highest-value change

**Produce one setting where acting on `η/L` yields a measured robustness gain over *both* shift-consistency *and* clean accuracy.** Concretely: find or construct a regime where clean accuracy and robustness disagree (across capacities, under corruption/distribution shift, or on a held-out architecture/task) and show the highest-`η/L` operator is more robust than the clean-accuracy-chosen one. This is the precise move that separates every corpus accept (xDxskDUvte beats the CNN, C36 beats RS, M9SK beats TRADES, dT0eNsO2YLu beats APS) from the rejects (aS2Y, KX5), and it would convert the paper from "rigorous diagnosis that explains a known correlate" into "a tool that adds value beyond what is free" — turning my 5 into a confident 6+. Absent it, the strongest realistic home for the work as a diagnosis-plus-reconciliation is TMLR or a workshop (the explicit recommendation that landed KX5hd1RhYP).
