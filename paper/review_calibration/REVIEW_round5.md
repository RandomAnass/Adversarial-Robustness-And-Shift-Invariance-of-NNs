# Review — "When Does Shift-Invariance Permit Adversarial Robustness? A Margin-Preservation Theory" (Round 5)

*Reviewer role: NeurIPS/ICLR area reviewer. Rating scale stated below. Calibrated against the corpus in `review_calibration/reviews/` (read in full: NeurIPS_2025_xDxskDUvte, ICLR_2024_C36v8541Ns, ICLR_2025_M9SKazbVkJ, ICLR_2024_kWS4iOkhXv, NeurIPS_2023_aS2Yl8s5OG, ICLR_2025_KX5hd1RhYP, ICLR_2022_O0g6uPDLW7, ICLR_2025_sOte83GogU; index for the rest).*

---

## Summary

The paper reconciles two opposing claims in the literature — that shift-invariance *reduces* adversarial robustness (Ge et al. 2021) and that anti-aliasing (a route to more shift-invariance) *improves* it — by arguing they are two regimes of one scalar: the margin-to-Lipschitz ratio `η/L` of the invariant feature. It contributes (i) an exact identity for the invariant linear margin as a projection separation (Thm 1) and a finite-group generalization (Thm 2); (ii) a Lipschitz–margin certificate `r₂ ≥ η/L` completed to a conditional two-sided bracket `η/L ≤ r₂ ≤ η/α` (Prop 1, proved App. A.2); (iii) a lemma that conv+quadratic+pool computes power-spectrum features (Lemma 1) plus reconciliations of Ge and Kamath; (iv) a lazy/NTK cluster theorem that invariance lowers the certified Lipschitz constant (Thm "lazy-cluster"); (v) a large empirical dissection — capacity-matched arms, three datasets, two threat models, scaling to PreActResNet-18, per-sample certificate, bootstrap CIs, gradient-masking checks — showing that threat-matched `η/L` predicts the robust radius / AutoAttack accuracy while shift-consistency does not (and anti-predicts under AT); and (vi) **new this round**, a structural/impossibility theorem (Thm 3): for bias-free degree-1-homogeneous nets, Euler's identity gives `‖∇M(x)‖ ≥ M(x)/‖x‖` pointwise, so `η/L` is capped by the input norm and "no first-order penalty can raise `η/L` without collapsing the margin," confirmed by four controlled interventions. The reframed thesis: a diagnosis **plus** an impossibility result for training `η/L`.

The central round-5 question is whether converting the standing weakness ("rigorous diagnosis without a method") into a *proven impossibility* changes the contribution verdict. My assessment: **it does not raise it. The new theorem is correct but its impossibility framing overclaims, and as a contribution category it repackages "no method" rather than resolving it.** The paper remains a borderline, accept-if-championed submission.

## Strengths

- **S1 — Reconciles a real, citable contradiction.** Placing Ge (2021) and the anti-aliasing line on one axis (`η/L`) is a genuine conceptual contribution, not a restatement. The Ge recovery (`γ_inv = 1/√d`), the power-spectrum sign-blindness argument, and the bispectrum escape are clean and correct.
- **S2 — Strong, well-controlled empirics.** Capacity-matched arms with identical parameter counts; AutoAttack ≤ matched PGD checks for gradient masking; per-sample certificate (Spearman 0.91–0.95, positive in all 36 models); bootstrap CIs + permutation tests; two threat models; scaling to PreActResNet-18 with a *second* exact-invariance mechanism (adaptive polyphase). This breadth directly answers the "limited evaluation" critiques that sank borderline papers in the corpus (cf. kWS4iOkhXv, aS2Yl8s5OG).
- **S3 — A useful, somewhat counterintuitive negative finding.** "Shift-consistency does not predict robustness — and anti-predicts under adversarial training — while threat-matched `η/L` does" is more actionable than typical critique results, because shift-consistency is a *widely used* invariance diagnostic. The threat-matching insight (`ℓ₁` gradient for `ℓ∞` training) is a real refinement.
- **S4 — Honest scoping.** Conditional converse labeled conditional; Kamath labeled "an interpretation, not a theorem"; smooth-activation caveat for the ReLU NTK case; Bubeck–Sellke caveats (global vs local Lipschitz, label-noise regime). The paper engages the single most relevant recent accept (wang2025bridging = NeurIPS_2025_xDxskDUvte) and positions it precisely as "necessary but not sufficient."
- **S5 — The four-intervention loop is methodologically clean.** Theory predicts collapse → all four first-order penalties collapse the margin, with no gradient masking. Falsifiable and confirmed; this is rarer than it sounds.

## Weaknesses (ranked, located)

**W1 — The round-5 upgrade does not resolve the standing weakness; it restates it. [§1 contributions; §3.6; §5 interventions]**
The prior weakness was "rigorous diagnosis without a method." The response is not a method but a proof that no method (via `η/L`) can exist. Per the calibration, this is the *exact* profile the field gates against: NeurIPS_2023_aS2Yl8s5OG (an honest, "exemplary"-presentation analysis paper with surprising findings but no improvement) was **rejected** with two 7s because "no reviewer stepped forward to champion" a "marginal" contribution; ICLR_2025_KX5hd1RhYP (a correct, first-of-kind *negative* result) was **rejected** as "technically limited / too narrow / better as a workshop." A results-focused reviewer will still read "no method" as the ceiling, and an impossibility result that does not change practice does not move that ceiling. The accepts in the corpus (M9SKazbVkJ, C36v8541Ns, sOte83GogU, xDxskDUvte) *all* pair insight with either a method that improves a metric or a positive scaled mechanism.

**W2 — Theorem 3 is correct but folklore-level, and the "impossibility / cap / lockstep" reading overclaims. [§3.6, proof at the Euler step]**
The math (degree-1 homogeneity ⇒ Euler `⟨∇M,x⟩ = M` ⇒ Cauchy–Schwarz `‖∇M‖ ≥ M/‖x‖`) is right, but it is textbook for bias-free homogeneous nets (the basis of the Lyu–Li / normalized-margin implicit-bias line). Like the ACR unboundedness in KX5hd1RhYP — which a reviewer dismissed as "a direct consequence of `Φ⁻¹`'s bounded domain" — this will read to a sharp reviewer as a three-line identity dressed as a theorem. More seriously, the *consequence* is overstated on two counts:
  - *The cap is not binding.* `η/L ≤ sup_i ‖x_i‖` ≈ tens for normalized CIFAR, while measured `η/L` is ~0.08–0.15 (`ℓ₂`, standard) or ~1.1 (`ℓ₂`, AT). The envelope floor `M/‖x‖` ≈ 7.85/27 ≈ 0.3 sits ~250× **below** the measured `‖∇M‖₂ = 81.9`. So the envelope leaves enormous room to lower `‖∇M‖` *without* lowering `M`: Theorem 3 does **not** forbid raising `η/L` in the operating regime.
  - *The lockstep only binds at Cauchy–Schwarz equality* (`∇M ∝ x`), which trained models do not satisfy — adversarial training aligns gradients with *semantic* features, not with `x`. So "lowering `‖∇M‖` must lower `M` in lockstep" is true only at the envelope, which the models are far from.
  The genuine no-go is carried by **Lemma 2** (scale degeneracy → constant classifier, itself attributed to Tsuzuku 2018) plus the **four empirical interventions** — not by Theorem 3. The abstract/intro phrase "we prove this coupling is structural… turns the `η/L` diagnosis into an impossibility result for training it" is therefore an overclaim: what is *proven* is a loose ceiling and a pointwise envelope; the *impossibility of training* `η/L` is shown empirically.

**W3 — Theorem 3's hypotheses are violated by every network in the paper's own experiments. [§3.6 caveat vs §5]**
The theorem requires bias-free, BN-free, degree-1-homogeneous nets; the experiments use PreActResNet-18 with batch norm and biases, which the paper itself says "break Euler's identity." So the headline structural theorem neither binds quantitatively nor applies to the headline models. This is the same mismatch the xDxskDUvte reviewers flagged (proofs assume pure equivariance / differentiability, experiments use hybrids) — there it was survivable because the paper also delivered a positive architectural method and scaled wins; here the theorem-to-experiment gap is left to the Bubeck–Sellke *remark*, which the authors honestly down-rank to "capacity context, not a tight account."

**W4 — Much of the framework is assembled from known ingredients. [§3.2–3.4, App. A.2]**
The Lipschitz–margin certificate is classical (Hein 2017, Tsuzuku 2018, by the authors' own statement); the two-sided bracket is conditional on a co-Lipschitz hypothesis that essentially assumes a reaching path with a lower-Lipschitz bound (close to assuming the conclusion); the lazy-cluster theorem rests on Elesedy's projection + smooth activation and is explicitly open for ReLU; the Kamath reconciliation is "an interpretation." The genuinely new theory pieces (Thm 1 hull form, Lemma 1 span, the orbit-rank propositions) are modest and the authors say so.

**W5 — Presentation density. [Abstract; §1]**
The abstract is a single ~400-word run-on; the contributions stack jargon ("co-Lipschitz," "orbit-closed," "threat-matched," "self-symmetrizes," "rich/lazy regime") before any are defined. The C36v8541Ns thread shows reviewers penalize exactly this ("verbose… I struggled a lot to understand"); that paper still got in, but only after heavy rewriting. The tables/figures are clear; the prose is the bottleneck.

**W6 — Statistical thinness in places. [§5 power paragraph]**
Several headline correlations are over 8–12 cells; the standard-training consistency CI is `[-0.68, +0.09]` (not significant), the AT consistency anti-prediction CI is `[-0.92, -0.02]` (barely excludes 0), and the four-capacity-matched-arm anti-prediction is `-0.43` (n.s.) — it "sharpens to -0.81 only once the two low-invariance arms widen the range." The effect is real but rests on widening the design until it appears, which a skeptical reviewer will probe. Single-author, no ImageNet (acknowledged).

## Questions

1. **Quantify the envelope slack.** For the trained models, what is the per-sample distribution of `‖∇M(x)‖ / (M(x)/‖x‖)`? If it is ≫ 1 (as the Table 1 means suggest, ~10²), then Theorem 3's envelope is far from binding and the lockstep is not forced — please reconcile this with the "no penalty can lower `‖∇M‖` except by lowering `M`" claim. What actually forces the collapse: the envelope, the scale degeneracy (Lemma 2), or optimization/capacity?
2. **Theorem-to-experiment gap.** Since the ResNet models have BN and biases (Euler broken), is there *any* version of Theorem 3 that holds for them, even approximately? Can you measure the Euler residual `⟨∇M,x⟩ − M` on the trained models to show how far the identity is from holding?
3. Is there a held-out demonstration that selecting an operator by `η/L` (vs by shift-consistency) yields a *real* robustness gain on an unseen task/architecture? That single experiment would convert the diagnosis into something a practitioner can act on.
4. The interventions raise `η/L` (the `ℓ₁` gradient norm falls 38→22) yet AutoAttack is flat-to-down. Is that because `η/L` was raised by shrinking `‖∇M‖` *with* the margin (your claim), or because the threat-matched `η/L` was already a *predictor across architectures* but never a *causal lever within one architecture*? These are different failure modes; which does the data support?
5. The bracket's co-Lipschitz `α` along a "reaching path": for the trained ResNets, can you exhibit `α` (hence `κ`) empirically, or is it only available for the linear/power-spectrum surrogates?

## Scores

- **Soundness: 3 (good).** The empirics are carefully controlled and the proofs are individually correct. The one real soundness issue is interpretive: the impossibility framing of Theorem 3 is stated more strongly than the theorem supports (W2), and the theorem's assumptions do not cover the experimental models (W3).
- **Presentation: 2 (fair).** Logically structured with clear figures/tables, but the run-on abstract and jargon-front-loaded intro materially impede a first read (W5).
- **Contribution: 2 (fair, bordering good).** The empirical reconciliation + diagnostic is a solid "fair-to-good" contribution; but the round-5 headline (a folklore identity reframed as impossibility) adds narrative coherence without adding an actionable contribution, and overclaims its reach. As a package it is a diagnosis + negative results with no method.

## Overall rating

**5 / 10 — marginally below the acceptance threshold (ICLR 1–10 scale; 6 = marginal accept, 5 = marginal reject, 8 = accept).**

This is consistent with the paper's stable round-1–4 borderline-5 position, and it is my judgment that **Theorem 3 does not move it.** The submission is clearly richer than the two nearest rejected analogs (aS2Yl8s5OG honest-analysis-no-method; KX5hd1RhYP folklore-based-negative-result) — broader experiments, a genuine literature reconciliation, a useful diagnostic — so it should not fall below 5. But the contribution category that top-venue results-reviewers gate on (a method, or a practice-changing result) is still absent, and the new theorem both overclaims and fails to apply to the paper's own models. A reviewer who values clean diagnoses and tolerates "an analysis paper need not have a method" (the M9SKazbVkJ / C36 spirit) could reasonably go to 6; a results-focused reviewer (the aS2Y / KX5 outcome) will hold at 5 or below. Without a designated champion this lands on the reject side of the borderline.

**Confidence: 4 / 5.** I checked the Euler/Cauchy–Schwarz derivation, the bracket proof, the lazy-cluster proof, and the table numbers, and I am familiar with the Ge/anti-aliasing/Lipschitz-margin literature. I am not certain how a panel would weigh the reconciliation contribution against the missing method.

## Decision and top-3 fixes

**Decision: borderline reject (accept-if-championed).** The honest impossibility reframing is intellectually attractive but, on inspection, repackages the standing "no method" weakness rather than resolving it; the new theorem is folklore-level and its impossibility reading is not supported in the operating regime.

1. **Walk back the "proven impossibility."** State plainly that Theorem 3 is a loose cap (`η/L ≤ sup‖x‖`, ~tens, far above the operating `η/L`) and a pointwise envelope whose lockstep binds only at `∇M ∝ x`; attribute the *training* no-go to Lemma 2 + the four interventions, with Theorem 3 as an idealized mechanism. Add the Euler-residual measurement on the BN/bias models and acknowledge the theorem does not literally cover them. Honest down-scoping here will *strengthen* credibility (the rest of the paper is admirably scoped).
2. **Deliver one actionable consequence.** Either (a) show `η/L`-based operator selection beats shift-consistency-based selection by a real robustness margin on a held-out task/architecture (turning the diagnosis into a usable tool — the lever that separates the corpus accepts from the rejects), or (b) prove a *regime-matched* lower bound that actually binds at the operating point. Absent this, the paper remains diagnosis-only.
3. **Rewrite the abstract and contributions as plain prose.** Two-sentence legible statement of the thesis; define `η/L`, "threat-matched," and "exact-cyclic" before using them; cut the 400-word run-on. Apply the same scope-honesty already used for Kamath ("interpretation") and the bracket ("conditional") to Theorem 3's reach.

**Best home if it stays diagnosis-only:** a strong TMLR or workshop paper (the explicit recommendation that landed KX5hd1RhYP and, in spirit, aS2Yl8s5OG). To clear a top-tier main track, it needs fix #2.
